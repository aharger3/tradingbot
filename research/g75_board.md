## Finding 1 — the engine is 40 minutes late, and I can name the reason

**It is one reason, and it was the same reason all seven times.**

Break-and-retest trades a level that is already drawn before the bell — yesterday's high,
yesterday's low, premarket high, premarket low. The engine can fire the moment price does the
dance. That is why it lands on your minute: on your seven break-and-retest cards its median
was **your exact minute**.

The one-candle rule has no such level. Its "level" is a candle the session has to *make
first*. On all seven of those cards, **the candle the engine traded off had not happened yet
when you pulled the trigger** — a median of **37 minutes** later. Once it printed, the engine
took three more minutes. That is your 40 minutes: 37 of waiting for the level to exist, three
of confirming it.

In your first half hour, **four bars in every ten the one-candle rule is not rejecting
anything — it has nothing to look at.** After 10:00 that is one bar in a hundred.

It is real beyond your seven cards: across all 4,508 trades in the book the one-candle rule
enters **23 minutes later** than break-and-retest, and it is the later of the two on 77% of
the days where both show up. Your seven cards exaggerated the size. They did not invent it.

**Why I am telling you not to fix it.** Your early one-candle-rule trades already in the book
win *more often* (50.5% vs 41.0%) and make **less money per trade** ($525 vs $748). And if you
delete every late one — the harshest possible version of "early or not at all" — your
one-trade-a-day book **does not move by a single dollar**: same 499 trades, same $721 a day,
same 25 of 25 months, same $5,993 hole. The one-candle rule is your first trade of the day on
**9 days out of 499**. Break-and-retest is the other 98%.

## Finding 2 — "the day, not the setup" is true about your eye and unavailable before the bell

**It reproduced, and then it got stronger.** Your yes-days scored 0.145 for trendiness, your
no-days 0.072. And it is not a 30-card fluke: across **all 812 days you have ever graded** it
holds at 0.133 against 0.101, with odds against chance of less than one in ten thousand. Your
own words agree — the three cards you tagged **"chop"** score 0.072 against a 0.123 average.

The way to do it by eye: **how far it got ÷ how far it walked.** First close to last close,
divided by the sum of every one-minute wiggle ignoring direction. A day you like keeps about
**one step in seven**. A day you refuse keeps **one in fourteen**.

**Why it cannot be traded.** That number is measured on the *finished* chart. To use it you
would have to know at 9:29 how the morning is going to go. I tried nine ways — premarket in
three windows, yesterday's same window, the last five days, yesterday's last hour, the daily
chart over ten and twenty sessions, the overnight gap. **Every single one is a coin flip**, on
your 30 cards and on all 812 days alike. Two of them lean *backwards*: your best days come
slightly more often on stocks whose recent daily chart was **less** trendy.

Priced on two years, the best honest version is **+$46 a day with an error bar from −$41 to
+$140**, and it costs you **39 of the 163 S-days the engine currently finds** — on the gate you
are furthest from. Even with perfect hindsight the ceiling is +$253 a day and it finds only 60
of your 278.
