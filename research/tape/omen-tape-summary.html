Where the money went

The reconcile walked from the lab rig's $4,569 a day down to the shipped book one change at a time. From the verdict, verbatim:

The money is lost at step 2 — where the lab's exit is replaced by the real engine's trade management — because the $4,569/day was earned by a stop that only fired on a candle close, so every wick through the level and back was a free pass; the real engine's 1R hard stop rests on the level and fills on that wick, which turns about one trade in twenty from a +2R win into a −1R loss (win rate 38.8% → 33.6%, counted as winners ÷ all filled trades: 5,556 of 14,327 → 4,820 of 14,332, no scratches in either book; average win and average loss unchanged) and takes $4,420 of the $5,550, and the scale-out ladder takes the remaining $1,131.

In trading terms: the stop that governs this book is the wick, not the close. That is his 1R ruling and it stands; it is also where most of the lab book's money went.

Five rules measured, none shipped

A rule ships only if green months do not fall and $/day falls no more than 5%, on both halves of the tape. Holding is a normal outcome.

  • heldThe 1R first-target rule (skip the trade when the day's high or low is less than 1R away): -$52 to $28 a day, 11 to 14 green on 732 trades, but the second half of the tape fell more than the rule allows.
  • heldThe 84% re-entry as decided on the call: -$52 to -$57 a day, 11 to 11 green on 769 trades; the first half fell.
  • heldThe one-candle-rule entry only when the retest candle is strong: -$52 to -$58 a day, 11 to 10 green on 764 trades; the first half fell.
  • heldThe 15-minute structure trend test: -$52 to -$61 a day, 11 to 10 green on 768 trades; the first half fell.
  • heldUp to three trades a day, stop after a win or two losses: -$52 to -$52, 11 to 11; it is already the unit the tape is scored on, so it changes nothing.

Every one of these moved less than its own error bar. None is a winner or a loser; each is a count.

How to read the tape

Open research/tape/omen-tape.html in the repo. It opens on this exact baseline: core-11 pool, honest close fill, his day policy, 769 trades, -$52 a day, 11 of 25 green. Clear a filter to widen the pool or change the unit; the phantom fill column is always beside the honest one. Filters: symbol, pool, setup, both grade ladders, month, week, weekday, entry time, level, fill mode, exit model, each measured rule on or off, instrument, day policy.

One known wrinkle: the engine can fire the same price under two level names in the same minute, so the tape carries 57 repeated rows inside this unit (1,020 rows, 963 unique by symbol, day and entry minute), worth -$2,514 of the total. It is disclosed, not fixed; fixing it is an engine change that goes through the gate like any other.
