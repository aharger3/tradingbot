**Population note: `Exit Efficiency` is populated on 136 of 350 rows (38.9%), and the
gaps are not random.** It is blank on all **170 losses** (170/170) and on **44 of 180
wins** (24.4%). Every one of those 44 blank wins has `Realized RR == Reward Ratio`
exactly — a trade that closed on its own intended target, the price he actually got WAS
the best price on offer, so TradeZella leaves the ratio undefined rather than print a
trivial 100%. Read together: **136 wins gave back something measurable (mean 39.6%
captured of what was on offer), 44 wins captured effectively 100% by construction, and
170 losses have no "give-back" to measure because the trade never had proceeds to be
efficient about.** The 39.6%/36.8% figures above are therefore a lower bound on how
efficient his *winners* are as a class, not a number that folds in the 44 perfect exits.

The full-book aggregate ratio (37.8%) sidesteps that population split — it sums realized
R and MFE R across all 349 scorable rows (losses included, same denominator convention as
the engine's 21.9%) — and it lands within 2 points of the native column's mean. **Two
independently-built numbers agree it's high-30s%, and that is materially above the
engine's 21.9%.** The gap (roughly +16 points of MFE capture) is the size of the prize:
narrowing the engine's give-back toward his rate, on the engine's own 2-year book (mean
MFE +3.8436 R), is worth on the order of +0.6 R of mean per trade if it fully closed.

### Does his own book agree with what he told me?

**Partially, and the disagreement is the more useful half.** His stated shape was full
size to ~10:30, 40-50% to 11:00, ≤10% runner past 11:00 or nothing. Two reads:

- **The book agrees that most of the edge is realized early.** Median best-exit gap is
  29 minutes; 58.3% of best exits land at or before 10:30. That is consistent with "full
  size until 10:30" being where the bulk of his opportunity actually sits, not a
  conservative floor he never gets to use.
- **But a full quarter of his best exits (25.1%) land AFTER 11:00** — far above the
  "≤10%, or nothing at all" he described for that window. If his best-exit tick is past
  11:00 on a quarter of his trades, either the runner he described is doing more work
  than "10% or nothing" implies, or he is (by his own description) leaving a real share
  of his best-available exits on the table by cutting most size at 11:00.

**Cross-referenced against `research/w2_time_ladder.md` (`52b82d9a`): the two rigs point
the same direction, not opposite ones.** W2 found the clock step time is "nearly free"
(10:15/10:30/10:45 spread only 0.0533 R) but the BACKSTOP costs money — holding the 10%
runner from 11:00 out to 16:00 cost -0.0132 R of mean and bought nothing on the median
(§2: "his instinct that past 11:00 it is '10% of the trade, if or nothing at all' is
confirmed on the tape, and this book's answer is 'nothing at all'"). W11's timing read is
consistent with that: most of HIS best exits already happen by 10:30, so a policy that
force-flattens hard at 11:00 is not fighting his own realized opportunity — it is
following it. The 25.1%-past-11:00 share is not evidence to hold more past 11:00; it is
evidence that on those specific trades the runner should have been let go further than
"≤10%", which W2's family does not model (W2 sweeps clock+rung shape, not a variable
runner size).

## 3. What is his actual target discipline?

**He is a fixed-target trader, and the target is ~2.0R, not W2's 2.5R optimum.**

`Reward Ratio` = `Initial Target` / `Trade Risk` (verified: row 0, `1192.5 / 592.5 =

per trade, set before the outcome is known.

**Two-thirds of every trade in his book (235/348) is intended at 2.0R, and the whole
distribution's p25–p75 sits inside [1.99, 2.14].** This is much tighter than "flat_2r
behaviour" in the loose sense — it is Austin literally setting a ~2R target on almost
every single trade, not an emergent pattern. Only 17 of 348 (4.9%) target 2.5R.

**W2 and W6 do not converge on the same number, and that is worth knowing plainly.** W6
found his *realized* median (+1.4368) and near-zero 0-1R dead zone (3/349) "looks like
`flat_2r` behaviour" — correct, and this file confirms the mechanism: he is *targeting*
2.0R explicitly, not drifting there. W2 independently found `flat_2.5r` is the only exit
arm in its swept family that passes the every-month durability gate (median +2.5000,
mean +0.7537, 25/25 months) — and Austin's own hand-replayed intent does not land there.
**The two rigs disagree: his stated discipline targets 2.0R; W2's optimum on the engine's
2-year book is 2.5R.** Two readings of why that is not a contradiction to resolve blindly:
(1) his book is a 2-symbol, 1-setup, hand-selected sample with a different MFE shape than
the engine's 20-symbol, 6-setup book, so the two optima are not guaranteed to match; (2)
W2's own report already warns that a fixed target's median IS the target whenever win
rate clears 50%, so `flat_2.5r`'s durability edge over `flat_2r` is thin (25/25 vs 24/25
months, +0.7537 vs +0.6997 mean) — not necessarily large enough that Austin's own 2.0R
instinct should be read as "wrong" rather than "a different, also-reasonable point on a
fairly flat curve between 2.0R and 2.75R" (§4 of `w2_time_ladder.md`: the median only
craters past 2.75R). **Report both, resolve neither — this is a disagreement, not noise.**
