## Read

- **Nothing clears the 2.0R gate, and nothing beats the incumbent.** Best whole-book is `st_70_30_be / clock` at **+0.914R** against ladder B's +0.957R. Best on S is `st_30_70_be / clock` at **+1.267R** against +1.283R. Every structure-trail variant is *below* the incumbent on both slices.
- **Weight taken at the first rung beats weight left on the trail, monotonically**: whole book, clock arm, `st_30_70` +0.897 → `st_50_50` +0.906 → `st_70_30` +0.914, and the pure trail `st_100_trail` +0.827. The structure trail is a *worse* home for size than the HOD/LOD rung it is meant to improve on.
- **The far-target tail costs money rather than making it.** Moving 25% of the book from the trail to a 4R partial: `st_50_50` +0.906 → `st_50_25x4r_25` +0.855. Adding a 5R rung on top: +0.839. A trade that reaches 4R is in a trend the trail is still riding; capping it there books the smaller number.
- **Removing the 11:00 clock is worse for every single variant**, as it was in G7 — +0.914 → +0.887 on the best whole-book policy. Structure does not fix that: after 11:00 the swing trail gives back more than the extra room earns.
- **Break-even is nearly free on a scale-out and ruinous without one.** `_be` variants move mean R by <0.01R (they only bind on trades the trail was going to lose anyway) but win rate rises 1-3 points. On the rungless `st_100_trail` the break-even floor costs -0.108R and 12 points of win rate: with no partial booked, break-even is a stop that has not earned its place yet.

## The ceiling of this family

Three hindsight bounds, none of them tradeable, all of them printed to say how much room an exit rule actually has.

- **`oracle_stopped`** — best CLOSE with hindsight, but the trade's own stop stays live and close-triggered. This is the honest ceiling: no policy that keeps Austin's stop and exits on closes can beat it.
- **`oracle_best_close`** — same, stop switched off.
- **`oracle_MFE`** — the best price the trade ever *traded* at. Nothing can beat it, and nothing comes close to it either.

## What would have to change

**The room is there and the exit cannot find it.** Inside the 11:00 window the stop-respecting oracle returns +3.501R — the gate is 2.0R, so the ceiling is not the problem. The incumbent captures **27.3%** of it; the best structure trail captures less. That is the whole finding: the R exists, and no rule in this family — ATR, prior bar, consolidation, and now market structure — knows which excursions to hold for.

The reason is separability, and it is measurable. Take the 473 signals the incumbent **loses** on: their stop-respecting oracle averages **+0.647R** (median +0.45R), and 33.8% of them offered a close at +1R or better before they died. A third of the losses are trades that went the right way first and came back — and while they were going the right way they looked exactly like the winners. An exit fired on price alone cannot separate them, which is why every unit of extra patience this rig bought (looser trail, no clock, far targets) came back out as give-back, and why the *tighter* rung took the money.

So the binding constraint is **information at entry, not management after it**. G7 said the exit is not the constraint; P10 says why — the exit family has +2.544R of headroom it provably cannot reach, because the signal that would tell it when to hold is not in the bars. Closing the gate needs either entries whose winners separate earlier (P1-P3, the entry-selection block), or an exit conditioned on something outside this book — the day's regime, the level being traded into, SPY alignment — none of which any policy here reads.
