### The rr inversion is real, and it is arithmetic, not edge

**Every single loss in this book is exactly -1.0000 R** (0 exceptions out of 474 losses -- the -1.25R floor never binds, `research/omen6_backtest_truth.md`). So the denominator of "avg win / avg loss" is the constant 1, and **rr IS the average winner**. It is not a measure of edge; expectancy is `W * rr - (1 - W)`, and that reconstruction is checked below.

| downgrade count | **n** | win rate W | avg WINNER (= rr) | avg loser | `W*rr - (1-W)` | actual mean R |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | **23** | 73.9% | +2.0591 | -1.0000 | +1.2611 | +1.2611 |
| 1 | **137** | 63.5% | +2.4249 | -1.0000 | +1.1749 | +1.1749 |
| 2 | **313** | 51.9% | +2.7483 | -1.0000 | +0.9467 | +0.9589 |
| 3 | **397** | 51.9% | +2.7647 | -1.0000 | +0.9538 | +0.9561 |
| 4 | **147** | 46.3% | +2.6574 | -1.0000 | +0.6919 | +0.6919 |

Read the two moving columns together. Between 0 downgrades and 3 the average winner grows +2.0591 -> +2.7647 (**+34%**, Austin's observation, and it is correct) while the win rate falls 73.9% -> 51.9% (**-22 points**). The second move is bigger than the first, which is why mean R goes DOWN over the same span. **A dirtier setup that still manages to win had to travel further to get there** -- the ladder is not paying more for worse setups, it is surviving less often and the survivors are further from the entry. rr is the wrong statistic to rank a ladder on, and it is the only statistic on which the ladder is inverted.

- `-rho` -- Spearman between the score and realised R, negated so bigger is better (a correct downgrade score is NEGATIVELY correlated with R).
- `lift` -- mean R of the cleanest bucket (score <= 0) minus the mean R of that half of the book. This is the number a selector would actually harvest.

### What is NOT measured here, and why

The 100-card held-out recall rig (`research/marks/probe_omen_test1_2026-08-27.jsonl`, 15 S / 27 A / 16 C / 42 X) is **not** scored per subset. Scoring it needs one full `backtest_2y.py` bar replay per arm -- that is what `research/r3_downgrade_grader_ab.py` does -- and this lane is a no-bars book rig. So no subset below may be quoted as buying held-out recall. A subset of a set that bought nothing is not entitled to an assumption that it buys something.

## 6. Why a ladder that looks right still fails as a selector

**The selection budget is already spent.** Of the 1,017 traded rows, **1,005 (98.8%) are `seq == 1`** -- the first signal of their symbol-day -- against 13,064 of 45,193 (28.9%) in the full signal population. Being first is very nearly NECESSARY to trade and nowhere near sufficient -- only 7.7% of the 13,064 first-arrivals reach the book. A grader applied downstream of that is re-ranking a population that has already been filtered on an axis the grader cannot see.

**The engine's own selector is BLIND to the downgrade count.** If the legacy grader and the downgrade count were measuring the same thing, the share of each downgrade bucket that reaches the traded book would vary with the count. It does not:

| downgrade count | signals in the book | traded | **selection rate** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1,005 | 23 | **2.29%** |
| 1 | 7,207 | 137 | **1.90%** |
| 2 | 14,179 | 313 | **2.21%** |
| 3 | 14,966 | 397 | **2.65%** |
| 4 | 7,836 | 147 | **1.88%** |

Selection rate spans 1.88%..2.65% across the whole ladder -- a 1.41x range against a mean-R range of 0.5692 R. The engine trades roughly 2 in 100 signals from EVERY bucket. The downgrade score and the thing that picks trades are close to orthogonal, which is exactly why bolting the grader on as a selector (R3) moved membership and not price.

**And the spread the ladder can harvest is small next to the spread other row facts carry.** Each row below splits the same 1,017 traded rows on one fact and reports the gap between the best and worst bucket's mean R.

## 7. The verdict, in Austin's own terms

**"3 downgrades has better rr than 1 downgrade and 0" -- TRUE on the rr reading and it is not an edge.** Average winner 2.765 R at 3 downgrades against 2.059 R at 0, a real +34%, but every loss in this book is exactly -1.0000 R so rr is just the average winner, and the win rate falls 73.9% -> 51.9% over the same span. Expectancy goes the other way: +1.2611 -> +0.9561.

**"that also might just be because the sample size is inverted" -- ALSO TRUE, and it is the bigger of the two effects.** n = 23 against n = 397. The 95% CI on the 0-downgrade bucket is [+0.5176, +2.1026], 1.5850 R wide -- 167x the carried +-0.0095 R error bar, and wider than the entire mean-R range of the ladder (0.5692 R). **Every one of the 10 pairwise CIs in the ladder overlaps.** No two rungs are separable at n = 1,017.

**So: the ordering is real in sign and unestablished in size.** Mean R falls monotonically across all five rungs -- that is the right shape and it is not inverted -- but only 15.7% of bootstrap resamples reproduce the full monotone ordering, and the one mean-R inversion that does exist (score 3 above score 2, +0.0298 R) has a p of 0.897. Reading either as a result is reading n.

**And on the question the ticket actually asks -- why a grader that looks right by profitability fails as a selector -- the answer is not "the ladder is inverted noise". It is "the ladder is real, small, and applied at a seam that is already occupied."** Section 6: 98.8% of the traded book is `seq == 1`, and the engine's selection rate is 1.88%-2.65% in EVERY downgrade bucket. Arrival order picks the book; the grade re-ranks a population arrival order already chose. That is exactly the shape of R3's result -- wiring the grader in moved MEMBERSHIP (958 rows traded by both arms, 1 with a different R, `research/r3_downgrade_grader_ab.md`) and bought zero held-out recall.

### What follows for the simplification Austin asked about

Yes, it can be simpler, and the simplification is in the VARIABLES, not the ladder:

1. **`break_then_rejection` -- delete it.** 0 trips in 45,193 signals in two years. It has never once changed a grade. Fifth member of the unreachable-rule class (`research/p8_scratch.md`, `research/g10_arming_funnel.md`). Deleting it is provably free: the book cannot change.
2. **`level_not_respected` -- drop it or invert it, do not retune it.** Wrong-signed here and in `research/a1_threshold_sweep.md` and `research/w9_downgrade_signs.md`; it is the highest-trip-rate variable in the set, so it is the single biggest contributor to the C bucket while pointing the wrong way. `w9` warns that removing it alone breaks the count ladder's monotonicity -- that is the ladder's cosmetics, and section 4 shows the out-of-sample ORDERING does not need it.
3. **4 of the 8 are deletable without losing out-of-sample ordering power**, worst case across all four greedy runs. `no_displacement` is the one variable in every winning subset and by itself reaches >= 90% of all eight on three of the four runs.

What is NOT supported by anything here: changing the confluence weight (section 5 -- w = -1 has the best S-bucket lift of the four, and the whole-book -rho spread across w = -2, -1, 0 is 0.0087, which is smaller than the effect of any single variable), and expecting any of this to move held-out recall, which is not measured in this lane and which the full eight-variable version already failed to move (R3: 3/15 -> 3/15).
