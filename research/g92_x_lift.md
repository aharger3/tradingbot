# OMEN 8.0 R3 -- the targeted X lift

`2024-08-12` to `2026-08-11`, 29 symbols (MAJOR_15, INDEX_POOL, OTHER_POOL), 11923 symbol-days for the book. Recall is scored on Austin's own marked entries -- `research/austin_marks_v7.jsonl`, `austin_tier == "S"`, archived bars present: **130 S marks** over 118 symbol-days, split **temporally** into DEV (`day < 2025-09-01`, 69 marks -- where the regrade rule was checked) and **HELD-OUT** (`day >= 2025-09-01`, 61 marks -- the reported gate, never tuned on). A mark counts as recalled when the arm produces a signal within +/-3 bars of its `entry_i` (`t62_veto_autopsy.py`'s tolerance). All three arms come out of ONE run each of `backtest_week.simulate_day` at the committed omen-5.0 defaults (`STOP_ON_CLOSE=1`, `LADDER_MODE="B"`), so recall and book size are the same engine, not two rigs. $1,000 risk/trade. Nothing in `signal_runner.py` / `omen_bot.py` / `live_scanner.py` / `backtest_week.py` was modified -- the arms are monkeypatched inside this script.

Said plainly rather than buried: the strict between-ness the row asks for is a HELD-OUT result, not a property of every cut.

**What the rule actually touched.** Over the two-year book the targeted predicate saw 111129 X verdicts and regraded 29723 of them (26.7%); `on_all` regraded all 111131. Of the X verdicts it refused, 73117 had no FSM-named clean retest at that level (no registration, or the FSM tagged the level LATE/dirty) and 8289 were HTF-opposed (an X that never reached `_grade_pa` at all). Full breakdown by setup and by which CONFIRM-bar veto caused the X: `{'lift_br_at_level_only': 11850, 'lift_br_both': 5042, 'lift_br_colour_only': 8780, 'lift_ocr_colour_only': 4051, 'lift_to_A+': 239, 'lift_to_B': 5755, 'lift_to_C': 23729}`.

**Which of the rule's conditions do real work, stated against the obvious objection.** A reviewer should ask whether `targeted` is anything more than `on_all` with the trend veto left on. On THIS engine configuration the answer is partly no, and that is worth saying out loud rather than leaving to be found: `FVG_RETEST` and `FLAG_ENABLED` both ship **False**, so the only call sites that reach `grade_trade` at all are B&R, OCR and the 84% re-entry -- which means condition (a), 'only break-and-retest and one-candle-rule', refuses almost nothing today and would only start binding if either dormant setup were switched on. Condition (b) refuses 73117 candidates (the LATE/dirty-level tag does the work here; the exact-touch clause is inert while `DETECT_WIDE` is off, since with `retest_tol_mult = 0` the FSM already requires a real touch). Condition (c) refuses 8289. So the separation from `on_all` comes from (b) and (c) together, not from (a). The rule is still written with all three because it has to be correct under any configuration, not just this one -- but the honest reading of the numbers above is 'X-regrade on FSM-clean setups, with trend still a veto', not 'X-regrade restricted to two setup types'.

## Adversarial pass -- did the regrade just re-label losers as wins?

The row's own required check: price the trades `targeted` ADDS separately from the ones `off` already had. **New trades: 604, mean +0.4132R, 45.0% win rate.** Trades `off` already took and `targeted` still takes: 899, mean +0.9845R. Trades `off` took that `targeted` no longer takes: 27, mean +0.7260R (a regraded signal can claim a level or a dedupe slot ahead of a later one, so the arms are not perfectly nested at the trade level even though the grade rule is).

Setup mix of the new trades: {'one_candle_rule': 303, 'break_and_retest': 300, 'reentry_84_rule': 1}. Grade mix: {'B': 586, 'A': 18} (C-graded lifts are alert-only and never enter this book, so they do not appear here). The mix is overwhelmingly B, and that is structural: on the red-confirm-bar case the regrade's ceiling IS B, because `is_hammer_stick` / `is_inverted_hammer` -- the ladder's only route to A+ -- carry their own candle-colour requirement and still refuse a red long entry. The handful that reach A come from the OTHER veto: a correctly-coloured hammer whose confirm bar simply never traded back down to the level, which the FSM says it did three bars earlier.

For contrast, `on_all` adds 1474 trades at mean +0.6625R.

## Independent adversarial review (2026-09-03)

A separate reviewer (not the implementer) tried to refute all three headline claims and the mechanics behind them. **All three CONFIRMED, no bug found, no number changes.**

- **Recall ordering** (`off` 4/61 < `targeted` 5/61 < `on_all` 6/61): reproduced the report byte-identically, hand-verified both load-bearing marks against raw archive CSVs (AMD 2026-05-14 -- the FSM's retest bar IS the confirm bar, so `at_key_level` forcing is a no-op there, not borrowed evidence from elsewhere; HOOD 2026-04-13 -- confirmed its exclusion really is the HTF pre-veto, `PA._grade_pa` returns C but `PA.grade_trade(..., "bearish")` returns X, not a different bug), and swept the held-out cutoff date from 2024-06-01 through 2026-04-01: the strict ordering holds at every cutoff tried, far more robust than "one mark out of 61" suggests. It only breaks past 2026-04-14, when the sole `on_all`-only mark (HOOD) leaves the held set entirely -- expected, not a flaw. Also re-ran the regrade with the `LATE` clause removed (the one parameter chosen partly on book-wide, not held-out, statistics): identical recall on the identical 13 marks, book still passes 2x -- the one tuned knob is not load-bearing for either verify condition.
- **Book ratio** (1503 = 1.62x 926): arithmetic confirmed, and `off`'s 926 reproduced three independent ways, including a fresh run of `research/t8_two_year.py` itself -- which now prints 926/1263, not the 1017/1388 its own committed markdown reports. `t8_two_year.md` is stale with respect to today's `main`; not introduced by this row.
- **New trades not re-labelled** (604 new @ +0.4132R / 45.0% vs 899 retained @ +0.9845R): reimplemented the split from scratch off the raw rows, got an exact match; confirmed the 899 "retained" trades are byte-identical in outcome and R to their `off` counterparts (0 mismatches); confirmed the 27 displaced trades are a real, bounded effect (−19.6R total, not hidden); hand-derived the AMD loser independently (entry 447.05, stop 445.37 -- the FSM's own retest-bar low, target 450.41, stopped out bar 37 at 444.43, −1.00R) and separately spot-checked a *gained* mark (COIN 2025-03-06) that wins +2.86R, so the walked example wasn't cherry-picked toward either outcome.
- **Mechanics**: instrumented every `grade_trade` call over the full two-year book by call site -- all 29,723 lifts land on the four B&R/OCR sites, zero elsewhere (no scope creep); traced the FSM's retest-bar index and the `LATE` window and confirmed both are always at or before the confirm bar (no lookahead, not the R1 bug class recurring). The lift-cause and lift-destination category breakdowns both sum exactly to 29,723.

Two small imprecisions the review flagged, worth recording though they change no number: the `LATE` clause is narratively framed as central to keeping `targeted` narrower than `on_all`, but removing it moves held-out recall by zero and the book by only 78 trades (1.62x -> 1.71x) -- its refusals are real but not load-bearing on either verify gate; and "at_key_level forced TRUE" also silently forces the ladder's C rung, not just the A+/B ones, which is the correct reading of the stated rule but wasn't spelled out.

## Verdict

**plain:** the grader was throwing out trades for being the wrong colour candle, or for not touching the level on the exact minute it fired, when the setup's own detector had already watched price touch that level a minute or two earlier -- putting those specific ones back finds 1 more of Austin's S trades out of 61 held-out ones and takes the engine from 926 trades over two years to 1503, and the trades it adds make money on their own (+0.41R each) but less than the ones it already had (+0.98R), so it is a real recall gain bought at a real cost, not a free one.

**The middle exists, and it is where the row said it would be.** Regrading only FSM-validated break-and-retest and one-candle-rule setups moves held-out recall from 6.6% to 8.2% (+1.6 points) while `on_all` -- the same ladder with no setup or FSM precondition -- reaches 9.8%. `targeted` sits strictly between them, which is the whole claim: the recall the blunt arm buys is not all bought by ungating everything, and a narrow, structurally-grounded slice of it is available at 1.62x the book instead of 2.55x.

**But it is not a free lunch, and the report should not be read as one.** `targeted` collects 50% of the recall `on_all` buys for 40% of the extra trades `on_all` costs -- close to proportional. The case for it over `on_all` is therefore NOT that it is a better recall-per-trade deal on this corpus; it is that the trades it adds are ones a stated, checkable structural rule says the engine was wrong to refuse, whereas `on_all`'s extra 856 come with no account of why any particular one should have fired -- including counter-trend entries the trend rule vetoed on purpose. Whether that is worth 577 more trades a year is a decision, not a measurement, and this row does not make it.

**The added trades are priced separately and they are not carrying the result.** The 604 trades `targeted` adds pay +0.4132R at a 45.0% win rate, against +0.9845R on the 899 trades `off` already had. The regrade is therefore not re-labelling losers as wins: the new book is a separate, independently-priced set that stands on its own. Blended, `targeted` pays +0.7549R over 1503 trades vs `off`'s +0.9770R over 926.

**What this does not settle.** Recall is still nowhere near the 90% gate -- 8.2% held out is a lever moved, not a gate cleared -- and `on_all`'s 9.8% is the ceiling this whole family of fixes can reach, at 2.55x the book and +0.7834R. The X-veto is real and regrading it is worth doing, but it is not, on this measurement, the thing standing between the engine and Austin's S days on its own.

## The regrade rule, stated exactly

For a signal whose `signal_type` is `BREAK_AND_RETEST` or `ONE_CANDLE_RULE`, whose own detector has already named a specific bar at which price genuinely touched the level -- `detect_break_retest`'s ordered FSM (BREAK -> LEAVE -> RETEST -> CONFIRM, with the retest bar's own low/high proven to have reached the level, the FSM's `max_confirm_gap` of 3 bars already enforced, and the FSM's own `LATE` tag absent -- a level already broken earlier in the session is, in the detector's own comment, "dirty" and the entry "a LATE entry"), or `detect_order_block_setup`'s `wick_only` retest (the bar's low traded into the block, its body held clear) -- and whose `X` came from `_grade_pa` itself rather than `grade_trade`'s HTF pre-veto: re-run `_grade_pa`'s positive ladder with `at_key_level` forced TRUE and the confirm-bar colour gate dropped. Both of those vetoes are measured on the CONFIRM bar and only on the CONFIRM bar; the FSM already tested the same two things at the bars where they are actually decidable. Everything else -- the HTF veto, the minimum-risk D, OCR's 0.4% stop-width D, the PMH/PML cap, the session-extreme veto, level retirement, no-repeat entries, `_route`'s tight-stop test on C -- is untouched and still fires.

**Why those two and not others.** `omen-blockers.md` ("The grader throws away 93% of what the engine finds", off `research/t62_veto_autopsy.md`) counts the three D/X-causing rules on the signals that land on Austin's own marked entries -- **never traded back to the level 49, wrong candle colour 50, HTF bias opposed 35** -- and gives his rebuttal of each: *"The retest already happened up to 3 bars earlier and the FSM validated it. He enters intrabar, so a red entry candle on a long is normal. And he treats trend as one downgrade among eight, never a veto."* This arm takes the first two (99 of the 134 hits) and leaves the third (35). The first two rebuttals are claims about WHICH BAR the test is run on, and the FSM carries the evidence to run them on the right bar -- so they are in. The third is a claim about how much a trend disagreement should COUNT, which no FSM output can settle; regrading it would be a different row, and it is what separates `targeted` from `on_all` on the counter-trend signals. Keeping it out is the main reason this arm stays narrow.

**What makes it self-limiting.** The regrade cannot promote anything above what the grader's own ladder would have said with correct inputs, and the ladder's top rung (`is_hammer_stick` / `is_inverted_hammer`) still requires the right candle colour. So on exactly the case this rule exists for -- a red confirm bar on a long -- the ceiling is B. The rule cannot invent an A+. Downstream the engine's own committed rules still move the grade in both directions: the displacement gate caps a no-displacement B&R to C, and `_calibration_grade` floors the first with-trend C of the day back to B. Those are not part of the regrade and were not touched.

**How this rule was arrived at, including the part that was iterated.** The two CONFIRM-bar vetoes, the setup restriction and the HTF carve-out all came out of the code and `omen-blockers.md` before anything was run. The `LATE` clause did not: it was added after a first pass showed conditions (a) and (b) refusing nothing at all on the shipped configuration, which would have made `targeted` indistinguishable from "`on_all` with the trend veto still on" and would not have tested the row's actual question. It was chosen over the other available narrowing -- requiring the break leg to have displaced -- because `BNR_DISPLACEMENT_GATE` already ships ON and acts on the same signals downstream, so folding it into the regrade would double-count one rule rather than add an independent structural test. That decision was made on the book-wide lift counters, not on held-out recall; the held-out column was read once, at the end. A reader who wants the un-iterated version can set `"LATE" not in note` back to `True` in `br_wrap` and re-run.

## What could not be reconstructed

The row cites three sources for the size of the prize and none of them is in this repo. `g4_dropped_s` (the 7,219-of-7,485 figure), the string `on_all`, and W1's arm table (`on_all` 6/15 vs `off` 3/15 at a 12.5x book of 12,770 trades) appear NOWHERE in the tree -- not in `research/`, not on any branch, local or remote -- and neither does `research/t1_entry_minute_autopsy.md`, the 9-of-15 autopsy `omen-next-session.md` links. That is the same situation `research/g90_fill_arms.md` documents for `OMEN-7.3.md` / `g80_lookahead_refute.md`, and consistent with `omen-blockers.md`'s note that the work happened on a working copy that was never pushed. **So the verify criterion's specific numbers -- 52.5% `off`, 44.1% `on_all`, 12,770 trades -- are not targets here and were not aimed at.** They cannot be: the code that produced them does not exist anywhere runnable. This script builds its own `off` (6.6% held-out, 926 trades) and its own `on_all` (9.8%, 2359 trades) from committed code, and uses THOSE as the goalposts the targeted arm has to land between. Read the row's verify structurally -- between the two extremes, under 2x the book -- not numerically.

Two further gaps worth naming. (1) The vault's arm table is scored on 15 somethings (days, most likely -- T1's autopsy is over 15 days on which the engine reached his setup); this report's denominator is 130 marked S ENTRIES, of which 61 are held out. The two denominators are not the same object and the percentages are not comparable. (2) `omen-next-session.md`'s own 2026-09-03 flag -- whether `TRADE_FLOOR` cuts at detection or only at scoring, and therefore whether "SILENT: 0" and `omen-s-accuracy-100.md`'s "16 of 34 SILENT" are compatible -- is still unresolved and is NOT settled here. This script measures `backtest_week.simulate_day`, which has no `TRADE_FLOOR` at all; `live_scanner._tier` does, and it would cut into every arm's recall equally.

**On C alerts.** A share of what the regrade lifts lands on C, and C is alert-only in `live_scanner` (`_tier()` posts it as WATCH, not TRADE), so it never enters the P&L book. Recall was therefore measured both ways. On this corpus the two columns come out IDENTICAL for all three arms -- every mark any arm recalls, it recalls with a signal that is actually in the book, so none of the recall reported here is riding on alerts a human would have to act on manually. Either way, `TRADE_FLOOR` and `live_scanner._tier`'s `grade == "A+"` gate sit downstream of everything measured here and would cut into all three arms; reconciling those is T2's row, not this one.
