## The answer, in one paragraph

The 8 days it saw and threw away do not fail for 8 different reasons. **8 of them** die on the SAME test — *RISK FLOOR — stock risk < max($0.10, 0.15% of close)* — which accounts for 50 of the 58 discarded signals across them. It is written 2 times in the source — `signal_runner.py:2088` and `signal_runner.py:2328` — the call side and its put-side mirror. Everything else in this report is a footnote to that one gate.

**And the mechanism is already on record.** `research/g12_recall_regression.md` bisected it to `5e3677ea`, and `Specs/omen6-h2-master-spec.md` §3 W3 is the workstream chartered to fix it: `fill_price` back-dates the entry to the level on a bar that closes jammed against the session extreme, `intrabar_stop` then pulls the stop onto that same fill, and `stock_risk` collapses under `max($0.10, 0.0015 × close)`.

The rows below show it happening to Austin's own S days. The thinnest is **NFLX 2026-04-22 bar 60**, which books `entry == $93.71` against `stop == $93.71` — a risk of **$0.0008**.

And it is not only firing on junk bars far from the setup. On **AAPL 2025-09-11** the signal at bar 14 — the exact bar Austin marked, or inside the ±2 tolerance window around it — dies with **$0.1700 of risk against a $0.3419 floor**; 10 of the 50 signals this gate killed sit within ±2 bars of one of his own entry bars.


**The caveat that has to travel with this, and it is not small.** Recovering a signal on his day is not the same as recovering HIS trade. Across the 58 discarded signals on these 8 days, 49 are in the direction he took and only **11 are both in his direction AND within ±2 bars of his entry bar**. Opening the gate is necessary for those 11; for the rest it buys a fire on the right day at the wrong moment, on a sample where `research/t70_test1_score.py` already measures 12 false fires on his 42 refused days.

So W5's contribution is not a new suspect. It is that the suspect W3 is already prosecuting is confirmed **on held-out data**, and the constant it turns on — `B&R_MIN_RISK = 0.0015 × close`, `research/hallucination-audit.md` line 51, *"0.0015 multiplier territory not swept, HIGH - gates grade D"* — is one of the 33 constants **Austin never stated**. It is ours, not his. Section 4 prices what opening it would cost.

## §3 — the 4 that produced nothing at all

Nothing was emitted, so there is no grade to attribute and the tracer has nothing to say. The question becomes how far up the break-and-retest FSM the candidate got, and `omen_bot.BR_FUNNEL` answers it per level per bar. The stages, in the order the FSM evaluates them:

| stage | what failed |
|---|---|
| `no_confirm_close` | the current bar did not CLOSE back through the level — step 4 |
| `adverse_wick` | the entry candle's wick against the trade > 1.5× its body |
| `no_break` | no candle ever closed through the level inside the 12-bar window |
| `no_leave` | it broke but never fully cleared the level — chop on the line |
| `no_retest` | it broke and left but never came back to touch the level |
| `stale_retest` | the retest was more than 3 bars before the entry candle |

Read the session totals with care: the FSM is called once per bar per level per direction, so `no_confirm_close` is the base rate of "this bar is not an entry bar" and dominates every day by construction. The informative stages are the later ones — `no_break`, `no_leave`, `no_retest`, `stale_retest` — and the per-bar window at HIS entry bar, which is the only place the answer can be.

**The population fire rate is the number to hold next to the held-out sample.** The engine fires on **33.3%** of all archived symbol-days. On the 100 cards Austin graded it fires on 20% of his S days and 29% of the days he refused — so on unseen data it is no more selective than picking a day at random, and slightly less likely to fire on his best days than on his worst.

`unlock` double-counts nothing within a gate but a day can appear under two different gates; it is a ceiling per gate, not a partition of the days.

**Reading the last column.** It counts days where the engine fired nothing and this gate killed at least one of the signals it did produce — i.e. the days opening that gate could turn from silent to firing. It is an upper bound on the recall a gate can buy and says nothing about whether those fires are good: on this held-out sample the engine already fires on 29% of the days Austin refused, so every gate opened here buys false fires at that rate or worse unless something else selects.

- Held-out beats in-sample. Every number here is on the 100 cards or on the archive; none of it is on the corpus the rules were fitted to.
