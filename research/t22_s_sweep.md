## 3. The card — one tap, and what the optional second one is

Per card: a static SVG session chart (PDH/PDL, PMH/PML, ORH/ORL — no entry/stop marks,
because none are asked for), then:

- **Tap 1, required: `grade`.** S / A / C / none — Austin's ladder exactly, never the
  engine's legacy A+/A/B/C/X. This is the entire judgement the recall gate needs. One
  chip press finishes the card (`data-done` flips to `1` the instant a grade chip is
  pressed — verified in the test: "grade tap marks the card done").
- **Tap 2, optional: `downgrades`.** The eight countable variables from
  `omen-rulebook.md` ("settled 2026-08-23"), multi-select, `required=False`. Never
  auto-pressed — every chip on every card loads with `aria-pressed="false"`, checked
  directly in the test ("nothing pre-pressed on load," asserts
  `document.querySelectorAll('.chip[aria-pressed="true"]').length === 0` on a freshly
  built page). A card with only tap 1 answered is a complete, exported row.

No entry, no stop, no setup family. That is deliberate, not an omission — see §4.

## 4. The trap, and what this instrument physically cannot record

`research/build_omen_test1.py:696`'s bug was `out.entry_p = closes[i]` unconditionally
— the field that was supposed to record *where he entered* could not differ from the
bar's own close, so the page measured the bar, not him.

This instrument does not ask for entry or stop at all, so it cannot have that failure
mode by construction — there is nothing here standing in for a value he was supposed to
supply. The two fields it does record:

- **`grade`** — no default. The chip group loads with every option `aria-pressed=false`
  (verified above); the exported value is `null` until a tap happens, and the test
  proves it is independently settable to at least three distinct values across
  cards (`S`, `A`, `none`).
- **`ms`** — the per-card millisecond stamp, in scope §5. Its default is *absent from
  the export entirely* (no card produces `ms` until its grade chip is tapped), never a
  hardcoded number — verified by tapping three cards with real delays between them and
  checking the three recorded values are not all equal.

**What this instrument genuinely cannot record, and why that's the right cut, not a
gap in the thing being measured:** it cannot record *why* a card was graded S (only
*why not*, via the optional downgrades tap), it cannot record entry/stop/setup family,
and it cannot record confluence. Those are exactly the fields `build_omen_test1.py`
already asks for on a slower, richer card. Asking for them here would buy detail this
track's own goal ("the quicker you have a large sample size the better") explicitly
trades away. The one field genuinely novel to this page — `ms` — is scoped and limited
next.

## 5. The millisecond stamp — what it measures and its one real limitation

Implementation (`EXTRA_JS` in `build_s_sweep.py`, appended after `probe_page.shell()`'s
own script, never editing it): a single in-memory clock `lastAt`, initialized to
`Date.now()` when the page's script runs. On the **first** `grade` chip tap on any
card, `ms = Date.now() - lastAt`, then `lastAt` resets to that tap's time — so `ms` is
the gap between consecutive first-grade-taps, which on a card-by-card read-and-judge
flow is a real per-card latency signal. Persisted to its own `localStorage` key
(`omen-probe:s-sweep:_ms`) so a reload does not erase stamps already taken; re-tapping
a card's grade (changing his mind) does not re-stamp it — `ms` is fixed at the *first*
decision, not the final one.

**What it cannot record:** `lastAt` itself is not persisted across a reload — it is
re-seeded to `Date.now()` at each page load. If he closes the tab mid-sitting and comes
back, the next card's `ms` measures from the moment the page reopened, not from when he
actually resumed reading — a coffee break folded into "page reload" would read as an
artificially short first-card time on return, and background-tab time between a reload
and the first tap after it reads as unusually long. This is a known first-pass limit,
stated up front rather than discovered later: `ms` is a real per-card latency signal
*within an unbroken sitting*, not a lab-grade instrument. A v2 with an
`IntersectionObserver`-based dwell timer (start the clock when the card scrolls into
view, not at page load) would close this; not built here because it adds a second
timing source and this track's job was sample size, not instrumentation precision.

## 7. Delivery contract — checked against the 2026-08-22 failure

- **Saves as he works**: every grade/downgrade tap calls `probe_page.js`'s own `save()`
  — unmodified, so the same code path Test 1 already proved survives a reload.
- **Restores on load**: `restore()` — unmodified — repaints every chip's
  `aria-pressed` state from `localStorage` on document load. Verified in the test.
- **Visible save indicator**: `#saved` element, unmodified — flips to "saved" on every
  tap.
- **Export → Copy all / Download .jsonl**: unmodified drawer + buttons. `window.claude`
  is referenced exactly once in the whole page, inside `probe_page.js`'s existing
  best-effort downloads-capability probe, guarded by `try/catch` with the editable
  textarea as the fallback that always works — the same guard that already made Test 1
  safe against the 2026-08-22 finding that nothing persisted through the artifact
  runtime. This file adds no new dependency on it.
- **Static SVG, not canvas**: `probe_chart.render()`, unmodified. 0 `<canvas>` elements
  on the page (checked in the test), 250 real `<svg class="chart">` elements.
- **Works on a phone**: no pointer-marking of any kind on this page (unlike Test 1,
  there is no chart interaction at all — the whole card is chip taps), and the CSS is
  entirely `probe_page.CSS`'s existing responsive rules, unmodified.

## 8. Cost — seconds/card, cards/sitting, and what 250 cards moves

**Seconds/card: not measured.** No prior instrument in this repo is a pure one-tap
card, so there is nothing to read a real number from yet — that is exactly what §5's
`ms` field exists to fix on the *next* version, once this one has been used. For scale,
the two comparable instruments on disk: `build_probes.py`'s silent-day autopsy (3
questions/card, one of them multi-select) ran at roughly 24s/card by its own
docstring's "roughly six minutes" for 15 cards; `build_omen_test1.py`'s card (grade +
entry-bar tap + entry-minute tap + stop tap + setup tap, i.e. up to 5 taps) budgets
~30s/card ("~10 min" per 20-card part). This card needs one tap to finish. A defensible
planning estimate is **5–15 seconds/card** for the read-the-chart-and-decide time, but
this is a guess grounded in analogy, not a measurement, and is labeled as such here so
nobody downstream reports it as one.

At 5–15s/card, 250 cards is **21–63 minutes** total, unbroken. The page has no hard
part boundaries (unlike Test 1's 5×20 sections) — `probe_page.js`'s own progress bar
and "Next unanswered" jump cover pacing, and Export works on a half-finished page. The
footer text suggests stopping and exporting roughly every 50 cards, which at the same
rate is **~4–13 minutes/sitting** — short enough to fit between other things, which is
the whole point of cutting the card down to one tap.

**What single number 250 cards moves:** the held-out S-recall denominator, *if* the
newly graded S days are folded into the held-out-eligible pool (valid until whatever
grader is being scored is tuned against them — the same status the original 15 have).
This is a **projection**, not a promise — the actual S count depends on what he taps,
not on this script:

| | n (S denominator) | Wilson 95% CI at the observed 20.0% rate | width |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| today | 15 | [7.0%, 45.2%] | 38.1 pp |
| **projected**, 250 sweep cards folded in | ≈88 | [13.3%, 30.0%] | 16.7 pp |
| today, *if* recall were actually at the 90% gate | 15 | [70.2%, 98.8%] | 28.6 pp |
| projected, *if* recall were actually at the 90% gate | ≈88 | [81.7%, 94.5%] | 12.8 pp |

The ≈88 comes from `n₂ = 15 + 250 × (240/817)` — 240 S out of 817 total judged
symbol-days is the corpus S-rate T5's brief cites (its own denominator, not derived
here). 250 draws at that rate project **≈73 new S days**. This is an estimate chained
off a real, cited number, not a fabricated one, and it assumes the sweep's S-rate
matches the broader corpus's — a fair assumption since both are uniform random draws
over the same archive, but still an assumption, not a result. **The width roughly
halves either way the true rate falls.** That is the number this track buys: not a
verdict on whether the gate passes, but the first denominator in this project's history
large enough for a recall measurement to mean something narrower than "somewhere
between 7% and 45%."
