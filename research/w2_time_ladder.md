# W2 — the time-scaled ladder sweep

Generated by `research/w2_time_ladder.py` over the **1017** traded rows of `research/g3_arm_ow1.json` (the shipped arm, `ON_WATCH=1`, replayed by `research/g3_onwatch_2y.py` at `47e60796`), 2024-08-21 → 2026-08-21, 500 sessions, 28 symbols, `data_archive/` replay, zero fetches.

## The answer

**The median rises, the mean falls, and no arm in this family beats the incumbent on both.** The best median in the swept family is **R2  rungs 1/2R 50-40** at **+0.7969 R** against the incumbent ladder's +0.1192 R on the same rig at the same 11:00 horizon — **6.7×** the incumbent, and 71× the ±0.0095 R error bar. It costs mean R: +0.8976 → +0.5975. **19 of 20** swept arms beat the incumbent's median and **1 of 20** beat its mean.

**And a plain fixed target still out-medians the whole time-scaled family — `flat_2r` books +2.0000 R.** That number is a warning, not a result: a fixed target's median IS the target whenever its win rate clears 50%, so median R on its own is gamed by walking the target up until the win rate breaks. §4 walks it: `flat_2r` +2.000 (59.9% win) → `flat_2.25r` +2.250 (56.0% win) → `flat_2.5r` +2.500 (53.9% win) → `flat_2.75r` +0.487 (51.1% win) → `flat_3r` -0.247 (49.1% win). **Read median R next to mean R and win rate, never alone.**

**The one thing that beats the incumbent on the MEAN is the clock with no price rungs at all.** `R5 no rungs (pure clock)` books +0.9297 R mean against the incumbent's +0.8976 R and captures 22.7% of the tape against 21.9% — the best mean and the best capture in this whole report, beating even `hod_only`. Its median is -1.0000 R, because with no rungs most trades never take a profit and simply ride to a step. **So the clock is real edge and the R rungs are what convert it into a median**, and the two halves of §1.4's design are doing genuinely different jobs.

**The single most useful row in the report is not in the time-scaled family at all: `flat_2.5r`.** Median **+2.5000 R**, mean **+0.7537 R**, **25 / 25 months green**, 18.4% of the tape captured, +766.5 R total. **It is the only arm anywhere in this report that passes the DURABILITY gate** — every month green — and it beats `flat_2r` on median, mean, durability and total R simultaneously. It beats the incumbent ladder on median by +2.3808 R and on durability by 2 months, and loses to it on mean by 0.1440 R. Nobody had walked the fixed target between 2R and 3R before: `research/g7_exit_sweep.py` jumped straight from 2R to 3R and the useful ground is in between.

**The money gate is still not met and nothing here meets it.** The gate is mean R = 2.0 with every month green (`CLAUDE.md`). The best-median arm is +1.4025 R short on the mean at 24 / 25 months green; the best-mean arm is +1.0703 R short. Share of the tape captured FALLS 21.9% → 14.6% on the best-median arm — raising the median is bought by cutting the right tail, which is the trade this whole family makes.

### One rig, and every horizon named

Everything below is `exit_lab`. `backtest_week.py` runs an open position to the **16:00** EOD close while `exit_lab` force-flats at **11:00** (`CLOCK_BAR = 90`); comparing the two was a real bug found 2026-08-27 — 22 of 1,091 ladder trades booked more R than the 11:00 window ever offered. **The backstop is a swept parameter here, so it is printed in its own column on every row, and both controls are re-run at every distinct backstop. Two rows are comparable if and only if their `backstop` cells match.**

`share of mean MFE` is mean R divided by the mean maximum favourable excursion **at that row's own backstop** — a longer horizon offers a bigger ceiling, so the denominator moves with the row. `mfe_r` is imported from `research/h1_2y_nowatch.py`, unmodified.

Error bar: **±0.0095 R**, the narrow bar. The wide ±1.5799 R bar was RETIRED 2026-08-28 when Austin settled that a stop fires only on a candle close, and there is exactly one close per bar, so the 790-of-792 `intrabar_stop` class was never ambiguous (master spec §1.1). Every median delta in this report clears it by 5× to 260×; the small MEAN deltas inside the g7 family (§4) clear it by only 2× to 3×, and are called out where they appear.

Push the same comparison out to a 13:00 backstop (B7 vs B4) and the clock is worth +0.0815 R of median, so **the later the backstop, the more the clock matters** — which is exactly the regime Austin described when he said he holds past 11 with only a runner.

**The step TIME barely matters and the BACKSTOP costs money.** 10:15, 10:30 and 10:45 spread the median over just 0.0533 R (A3 / A1 / A4) — 15 minutes either side of his stated 10:30 is worth less than a twentieth of an R, so the parameter Austin was least sure of is the one that matters least. A5 is identical to A1 by CONSTRUCTION, not by coincidence: at an 11:00 backstop, "keep 10% from 11:00" and "keep 0% from 11:00" both flatten on the same bar at the same close. The runner is only a real parameter once the backstop is past 11:00, which is what B1–B5 test, and there it is worth negative R.

## 6. What this does not say

- **It does not reach the money gate.** Mean R = 2.0 with every month green is still +1.4025 R away. A better exit on the same entries cannot close that; the ceiling is the mean MFE of +4.0992 R at 11:00 and no exit captures a ceiling.
- **It changes no shipped default.** `time_ladder` is a new function; `exit_lab.scale_out`, `flat_target`, `CLOCK_BAR`, `MAX_LOSS_R` and `STOP_TRIGGER_BUFFER_FRAC` are untouched, and `--selfcheck` asserts `CLOCK_BAR` is back at 90 when the sweep finishes.
- **It does not beat `flat_2r` on the metric it was built for.** The best time-scaled median is +0.7969 R against `flat_2r`'s +2.0000 R. §4 explains why that comparison is not the win it looks like, but the number stands.
- **No arm dominates the incumbent.** Every arm that beats it on median loses on mean, and the one arm that beats it on mean (`R5`) has a -1.0000 R median. There is no free move anywhere in this family.
- **It is in-sample over all 20 parameter points.** Every point is chosen off the same data it is scored on, and `CLAUDE.md`'s rule stands: held-out beats in-sample. A held-out split of this sweep is the obvious next rig and it is NOT in this file.
- **It does not model options decay.** R is the result and dollars are a sizing skin (`CLAUDE.md`), but a 13:00 backstop on an 0DTE contract is not the same instrument as an 11:00 one, and this rig prices the stock tape only.
- **The rungs are R targets, not his structure rule.** The incumbent's tranche 1 exits on the causal-HOD rule. §1.4 says the rungs fire on R, so that is what was built; a time clock bolted onto the HOD rule instead is a different arm and is not swept here.
