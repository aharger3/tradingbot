# X1 — where the mean-R is actually lost

Generated by `research/x1_exit_attribution.py` over the **1017** traded rows of `research/g3_arm_ow1.json` (`ON_WATCH=1`, 2024-08-21 → 2026-08-21, 500 sessions, 28 symbols), replayed from `data_archive/` with zero fetches. Backstop **11:00** (`exit_lab.CLOCK_BAR = 90`) unless a row says otherwise. Error bar **±0.0095 R** (the narrow bar). Gaps: {'day': 0, 'bar': 0, 'index': 0}. `--selfcheck`: 0 failures.

Austin, 2026-08-28: *"just confirming the mean 2r issue is not after HOD/LOD scale, moving stop loss to break even? its all in the percent i scale and not holding it long enough?"*

## The answer, in one table

Each row is a same-book A/B — same entries, same stops, same tape, one lever moved — and the last column is **what the BEST available move in that bucket is worth**. The gap to close is +1.0449 R.

| bucket | Austin's question | the best move measured | what it buys |
|---|---|---|---:|
| (a) scale-out percentages | *"its all in the percent i scale"* | abolish scaling entirely — one unit, original stop, ride to 11:00 | **+0.0609** |
| (b) break-even after tranche 1 | *"moving stop loss to break even?"* | never move the stop (BE never) instead of BE at +1 R | **+0.1207** |
| (c) the 11:00 backstop | *"not holding it long enough"* | hold LONGER — the same ride, clock removed, run to the 15:59 close | **-0.1709** |
| (d) the target | *"is 2.5R medium better"* | flat 2.5 R instead of flat 2.0 R (and flat 5 R instead of 2 R: +0.2112) | **+0.0540** |
| (e) cutting losers faster | *"losers stop out quicker"* | the best of 15 / 30 / 45-minute time stops and a first-adverse-close cut (ride + 45-minute time stop) | **-0.0142** |

**Three of the five point the way Austin's sentence points and none of them is worth a fifth of an R.** The best of the 21 single-unit exit policies measured in this file is *one unit, original stop, no target, ride to 11:00* at **+0.9585 R**, against the shipped ladder's +0.8976 R at the same horizon. That difference — **+0.0609 R, 6× the error bar and 5.5% of the distance to the gate** — is the entire value of every exit lever in the brief, taken at its best. Against the book **as actually booked** (+0.9551 R, which runs winners to the 16:00 close) the best exit policy in this file is worth +0.0034 R.

Rows (a) and (b) are **not additive**: the (a) counterfactual is already a BE-never arm, so abolishing the scaling and abolishing break-even are the same +0.0609 R move counted once. Nothing in the five rows stacks with anything else in them.

**The shipped book is +0.9551 R as booked and +0.8976 R when `exit_lab`'s ladder is re-run at the 11:00 clock** (the book runs an open position to the 16:00 close; `exit_lab` force-flats at 11:00 — that is the whole difference and it is worth -0.0575 R). Every delta above is measured against the `exit_lab` arm so the entry, the stop and the tape are identical.

## 0. The ceiling — and the correction the ticket needs

This lane was briefed with *"the theoretical mean R of a perfect-exit oracle … NOBODY IN THIS PROJECT HAS COMPUTED IT"*. **That is wrong.** `research/p10_structure_trail.py::oracle_stopped` computed it on 2026-08-26 and published **+3.501 R** in `research/p10_structure_trail.md`. This file recomputes it from an independent bar loader (`p26.load_day`, archive-only) rather than repeating the table.

| ceiling | n | mean R | median | p75 | p90 | note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `oracle_stopped` — best CLOSE with hindsight, Austin's stop still live | 1017 | **+3.4986** | +1.96 | +4.95 | +9.23 | the honest ceiling for any close-exit policy |
| `oracle_MFE` — best price the trade ever traded at | 1017 | **+4.0992** | +2.50 | +5.41 | +9.75 | nothing can beat this |
| shipped ladder, `exit_lab` @ 11:00 | 1017 | **+0.8976** | +0.1192 | — | — | captures 21.9% of MFE, 25.7% of the oracle |
| **the money gate** | — | **≥ +2.0000** | — | — | — | — |

**The oracle ceiling is +3.4986 R, which is 1.75× the money gate.** So the gate is NOT unreachable at this entry quality on arithmetic grounds — the R exists on the tape. What is true is narrower and worse: an exit would have to capture 57.2% of a hindsight-perfect ceiling to pay the gate, and the shipped ladder captures 25.7%.

## 1. The gap to the ceiling, decomposed by trade class — this one IS additive

Every other decomposition in this repo is a set of A/Bs that do not add up. This one does, by construction: the per-trade give-back `oracle − incumbent` is summed over two disjoint sets and divided by the same n.

| class | n | share | mean R booked | mean R the oracle got | give-back per BOOK trade |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| incumbent WINS | 521 | 51.2% | +2.8172 | +6.2253 | **+1.7460** |
| incumbent LOSSES (≤ 0 R) | 496 | 48.8% | -1.1186 | +0.6344 | **+0.8550** |
| **whole book** | 1017 | 100% | +0.8976 | +3.4986 | **+2.6009** |

**67.1% of the give-back is on trades the incumbent already WINS.** The losers contribute +0.8550 R of the +2.6009 R gap — 32.9%. Cutting losers faster cannot pay for the gate even if it were free and perfect: the whole loser bucket is only 0.5456 R of drag on the book mean, so **a policy that made every losing trade vanish at zero cost would land at +1.4432 R and still miss the gate by 0.5568 R.** The winners' give-back is the only bucket big enough to pay, and §3 and §8 are why it cannot be reached.

## 2. Of the trades that ended ≤ 0 R, how many were green first

Under the shipped ladder at the 11:00 clock, 496 of 1017 rows book ≤ 0 R. MFE is stop-respecting and intrabar — the best the trade ever traded at before a close beyond the stop.

| touched | n of the losers | share of losers | share of the whole book |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ≥ +0.5 R | 380 | 76.6% | 37.4% |
| ≥ +1.0 R | 270 | 54.4% | 26.5% |
| ≥ +1.5 R | 190 | 38.3% | 18.7% |
| ≥ +2.0 R | 115 | 23.2% | 11.3% |
| ≥ +3.0 R | 43 | 8.7% | 4.2% |
| never green (MFE = 0) | 51 | 10.3% | 5.0% |

Loser MFE: mean **+1.3678 R**, median +1.1296 R, p75 +1.93 R, p90 +2.77 R.

**54.4% of the losing trades were up a full R first, and 23.2% touched the 2 R target and gave every cent of it back.** Only 10.3% never went green at all. This is the single fact behind every result in this file: a losing trade in this book is overwhelmingly a trade that WORKED and then failed, not a trade that was wrong from the first minute. P10 measured the close-based version of the same thing (33.8% of its losers offered a CLOSE at +1 R or better); this is the intrabar version and it is higher because a touch is easier than a close.

## 4. (b) Break-even, counted instead of swept

Two independent measurements of the same lever.

**The counterfactual.** One unit, original stop, ride to 11:00, stop moved to entry the moment price touches +1 R, against the identical ride with BE never. BE never +0.9585 R → BE at 1 R +0.8378 R, a cost of **-0.1207 R**.

**The count.** The BE stop actually fired on **549 of 1017 rows (54.0%)**. Of those, the tape then offered, AFTER the BE exit and before 11:00:

| the BE'd trade then reached | n | share of BE'd trades | share of the book |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ≥ +1.0 R | 398 | 72.5% | 39.1% |
| ≥ +1.5 R | 349 | 63.6% | 34.3% |
| ≥ +2.0 R | 300 | 54.6% | 29.5% |

**On the SHIPPED ladder** — `scale_out` traced rather than cloned — the runner's break-even floor is what ends the trade on **20 of 1017 rows (2.0%)**, and **3** of those (15.0%) went on to offer ≥ +2 R before 11:00. Runner exit reasons on the shipped ladder:

|---|---:|---:|

**Read the `t1_stop` row: on 447 of 1017 rows (44.0%) the ORIGINAL stop fires before tranche 1 ever reaches its HOD/LOD rung — the position never scales at all and never has a break-even stop to move.** Whatever the scale percentages are set to, they are inoperative on 44% of the book. Break-even is the binding exit on 2.0%. Between them, the two things Austin named as the suspected cause of the mean-R gap govern the outcome of 56% and 2.0% of the trades respectively.

## 9. What this does and does not say

- **The oracle ceiling is NOT below the gate.** +3.4986 R at the 11:00 clock, 1.75× the +2.0 R money gate. The lane brief asked for this to be flagged as the session's biggest finding if it came in under 2.0 R; it did not, and it was already published by P10 on 2026-08-26 at +3.501 R.
- **Austin's diagnosis is answered clause by clause, and it is NO on all three.** It is not the scale percentages — abolishing scaling altogether is worth +0.0609 R. It is not moving the stop to break-even — on the SHIPPED ladder the BE floor is what ends the trade on 20 of 1017 rows (2.0%), and where a BE rule IS imposed it costs -0.1207 R rather than earning. It is not the hold horizon — holding LONGER costs -0.1709 R and the best of the four faster-cut arms still costs -0.0142 R, so 11:00 is already at the top of that curve. Taken at its very best the whole exit family is +0.0609 R against a 1.1024 R gap: **5.5% of the distance.**
- **Where it IS lost: the winners' give-back, +1.7460 R per book trade, 67.1% of the whole distance to the ceiling** — and it is unreachable because the give-back is not separable. §8 shows winners and losers have near-identical adverse excursion (median MAE 0.63 R vs 0.88 R) and §2 shows 54.4% of the losers were up a full R first. On the way up a loser and a winner are the same trade.
- **Every arm here is a single-unit policy.** W13 already showed the only lever that raises aggregate R materially is scale-IN (+1.4697 R best), which multiplies the book rather than re-cutting it. Nothing in this file contradicts that; this file explains why re-cutting cannot work.
- **In-sample, every parameter.** The time stops, the BE triggers and the targets are all chosen and scored on the same 1,017 rows. No held-out split.
- **`exit_lab.hod_only` still has the off-by-one W13 §9 reported** (it can book below the −1.25 R floor on 5 of 1017 rows). It appears in §5 for continuity and is not load-bearing for any conclusion here.
- **Options decay, spread and fill are not priced.** R is the result, dollars are a sizing skin.

## The one next move

**Stop testing exits and take the 48.8% loss rate to the entry.** The arithmetic is now closed on both sides: every exit lever Austin named is worth +0.0609 R at its very best (5.5% of the gap), and even a hindsight-perfect loser filter — every red trade simply deleted — lands at +1.4432 R and still misses. The only bucket large enough to pay the gate is the +1.7460 R the winners give back, and §2 and §8 show it is not separable from price: 54.4% of the losers were up a full R and their median adverse excursion is 0.88 R against the winners' 0.63 R. **The book needs fewer, better trades, not a different way of leaving the ones it has.** `research/x1_mfe_mae.json` ships the per-trade MFE / MAE / oracle so an entry-selection rig can be scored against the path directly instead of re-deriving it.
