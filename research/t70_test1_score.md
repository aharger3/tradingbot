# T70 — OMEN Test 1 against the engine

Generated by `research/t70_test1_score.py` (`--selfcheck` green). Marks: `research/marks/probe_omen_test1_2026-08-27.jsonl` — **100 symbol-days Austin graded 2026-08-27**: 15 S, 27 A, 16 C, 42 X.

**This is the project's first clean held-out recall sample.** No rule was fitted on these days and no threshold tuned to them. Every recall figure published before this one — T4, T60, T61 — was measured on the corpus the rules were built from. Read this one as the out-of-sample number.

## Two ladders, and which maps to which

Austin grades **S / A / C / X**. The legacy engine grades **A+ / A / B / C / X** (`omen_bot.PriceActionAnalyzer._grade_pa`). The letters collide and the collision has already killed a rule: the rulebook's *"you need an A+ entry"* is his vocabulary, where A+ means what he now calls **S**, but the code read it as `_grade_pa`'s A+ — a different scale — and the 84% re-entry rule fired 3 times in two years.

The mapping used in every table below, stated so it is never silent:

| engine tier | his grade | why |
|---|---|---|
| `A+` | **S** | both are the ladder's top — zero downgrades, full size |
| `A` | **A** | his ladder has ONE rung between top and bottom; the engine has two (`A`, `B`), so both collapse onto his single `A` |
| `B` | **A** | as above — `B` is not a separate grade on his scale |
| `C` | **C** | both are the bottom tradeable rung (his: two downgrades) |
| _silent_ | **X** | the engine firing nothing IS its refusal; engine `X`/`D` is a skip, never a grade |

**`A+` never occurs in this sample** (engine tier mix across all 100 days: {'B': 21, 'C': 5, 'A': 1}), so the column that maps to his S is empty by construction. The engine's top tier is not something it reaches on Austin's days.

## The three numbers

`S recall: 3/15` — the engine fires at all on 3 of his 15 S days (**20%**). In-universe: **2/12 = 17%**.

`false fire: 12/42` — the engine fires on 12 of the 42 days he graded X, an explicit refusal to trade (**29%**). In-universe: **11/37 = 30%**.

`entry match +/-2 bars: 4/58 = 7%` over the 58 he graded S/A/C — a fired entry within +/-2 bars of his own entry bar (`t4_engine_recall.TOL`). In-universe: **3/47 = 6%**.

| metric | all 100 | in-universe 84 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **S recall** (fires at all on an S day) | 3/15 = 20% | 2/12 = 17% |
| tradeable-day recall (S/A/C) | 15/58 = 26% | 14/47 = 30% |
| day precision (of days it fired on, ones he'd trade) | 15/27 = 56% | 14/25 = 56% |
| **false fire** on refused (X) days | 12/42 = 29% | 11/37 = 30% |
| **entry match +/-2 bars** (of the 58 graded) | 4/58 = 7% | 3/47 = 6% |
| entry match, ANY signal incl. skipped (upper bound) | 17/58 = 29% | — |

### Verdict

**The engine is MORE likely to fire on a day Austin refused than on a day he called S** — 29% of his X days vs 20% of his S days (in-universe: 30% vs 17%). On unseen days the signal is inverted: it is not a weak detector of his setups, it is not detecting his setups.

The 27 fired days split 15 he would trade / 12 he refused. Of the 15 tradeable days it does fire on, only 4 put an entry within +/-2 bars of his — so the day-level recall of 15/58 = 26% overstates the agreement: the **bar-level** number is 4/58 = 7%.

`t4_engine_recall`'s in-sample conclusion — a **detection** problem, not a filter problem — survives the holdout. 4 of the 12 silent S days produce no signal of any grade at all, not even one the filter threw away; on the other 8 the engine saw something and dropped it. No gate on the trades it already takes recovers setups it never sees.

## Data quality on the marks themselves

- **`entry_p` is the entry bar's CLOSE by construction** (`research/build_omen_test1.py:696` writes `out.entry_p = closes[i]`). He picks a minute; the page writes that minute's close. It is not a fill price and nothing here treats it as one — see `research/p25_midcandle_entry.md`.
- **6 of the 57 stops he typed are not prices** and are refused, not repaired, by `p25_midcandle_entry.clean_stop` (a stop more than 50% from entry is a note, not a stop): AAPL 2025-12-11 `931`, UBER 2025-09-18 `121052`, MU 2026-03-19 `931`, AMZN 2025-08-22 `957`, MSFT 2025-04-11 `930`, TSLA 2026-01-08 `20`. That leaves 51 usable stops.
- All 100 symbol-days have archived bars; none of the silence below is missing data.

## Method

- Detection: `research/t4_engine_recall.run_day` — `signal_runner.SignalRunner.detect_signals` replayed bar-by-bar with `runner.candles = candles[:i+1]`, PDH/PDL/PMH/PML/HTF reconstructed from `data_archive`, one entry per setup idea per 30-bar window, 11:00 entry cutoff. Not reimplemented here.
- **Fired** = the entries the engine would take (grade A+/A/B, or C with a viable stop). D/X-graded and tight-stop-C signals are *skipped* by `SignalRunner._route` and are counted only in the any-signal upper bound.
- A day is *found* when it fires at least one entry — `t61_onwatch_ab`'s `n_fires > 0`. An entry *matches* when a fired entry bar is within **+/-2 bars** of his `entry_i` (`t4_engine_recall.TOL`).
- `ON_WATCH` left at the `signal_runner` default. No default, threshold or engine behaviour was changed to produce these numbers.
- His X (`grade_std: "none"`) is a judgement — he looked at the day and refused it — so a fire there is a false fire, not an unlabelled day.
